Gender Roles in Horror Films

Examining horror movies gives us a great lens to see how society views gender roles. I chose Night of the Living Dead and From Dusk Till Dawn because they both offer a unique look at these roles in pretty intense settings. Also, I’m not a huge fan of horror, but I am not scared of zombies or I’ll watch anything Quentin Tarantino is associated with.

In Night of the Living Dead, Barbra starts off as the typical scared girl, which feels a bit old-school and frustrating. She’s super passive for most of the movie, especially because she watched her brother “die” and just can’t handle it. It’s like she fit the old damsel in distress mold way too well. Ben, on the other hand, is the typical male hero, taking charge and trying to protect everyone. This setup feels like it’s reinforcing the idea that men do the saving and women need saving. It pretty much happens with all the female characters in the movie as well.

From Dusk Till Dawn starts off like a typical crime movie but then goes full-on vampire crazy, which totally shifts the gender role discussion. Satanico Pandemonium, despite being the vampire queen and super powerful, is also super sexualized, which is typical of the femme fatal we often see in horror movies. On the other hand, there is Kate, which feels like they put her in as a damsel in distress until she does a 180 and starts wasting fools with a crossbow in the finale. Character growth or another trope? Hard to tell.

Both movies play with gender in their own ways. Night of the Living Dead uses its settings and horror elements to challenge and sometimes reinforce traditional gender roles. Barbra’s breakdown can be seen as a realistic portrayal of trauma, rather than just making her weak. Meanwhile, From Dusk Till Dawn has fun with its roles, like turning a typical seductive performance into a scene of horror and empowerment.

Night of the Living Dead and From Dusk Till Dawn show that horror movies can both challenge and reinforce traditional gender roles. They reflect their times’ attitudes but also push against these norms in interesting ways. As a viewer, it’s interesting to see how these films play with expectations, and how they try to subvert them to make a statement about the industry and world at the time they were created.
