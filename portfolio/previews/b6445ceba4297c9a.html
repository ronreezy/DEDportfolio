2Critical Thinking Key Concepts - entire listUse the current list with 80% relevance and 90% peer-reviewed validity rules.Quality information: Evidence that can withstand scrutiny in your field. Credible resources: A source earns credibility when you can answer three things: who created it, why they know, and when. Diverse perspectives: The blind spot you cannot see is the one someone standing elsewhere can. Logical reasoning: The path from evidence to conclusion should have no gap a student cannot explain. Innovative approaches: When the manual runs out, this is what takes over. Cultural impacts: A decision that works for everyone in the room may not work for everyone affected by it. Relevance: Evidence counts when it directly supports the decision, claim, or task at hand. Feasibility: A great idea that cannot be resourced is not a plan. Efficiency: Getting the same result for less time, cost, waste, or risk. If it saves resources, show the math. Validity: Trust the method before you trust the conclusion. Ask how the evidence was produced. Inclusivity: Who does your design leave out? Name them. Then fix it. Disparate cultural impact: The same rule applied equally can still land unequally.Removes discipline-narrow percentages that could alienate technical faculty.
