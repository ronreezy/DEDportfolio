Artist Statement: Ron Lethcoe

Every so often, a band comes along and revolutionizes music. In the 60’s it was the Beatles. In the 70’s it was Hendrix. In the 80’s it was Duran Duran. And in the 90’s it was the Wu-Tang Clan. Nine rappers, from the hardest ghettos of the New York City boroughs, came together to tell the world how it was living life on the streets. Their lyrics were inspired by everything from kung fu flicks to comic book tricks. Their rhymes are like lyrical swordplay. They continue to inspire millions, year after year, and have a huge impact on my way of life.

The main source of inspiration for my current work is the Wu-Tang Clan. I consider their music a form of art. In my work, I wanted to take their lyrical swordplay and try to replicate it in a physical space. Their history and experiences as expressed in their music is the basis for ideas I had in creating this world. Each diorama is a scene to a story about how I interpret how these nine lyricists started out. References to songs, characteristics, and nicknames of each member individually and as a group are the subject of my scenes. With Wu-Tang, everything means something, and I tried to do the same for my work.

Here is an example of Wu-Tang’s explosive lyricism:

I bomb atomically, Socrates' philosophies and hypothesis can't define how I be droppin these mockeries, lyrically perform armed robbery Flee with the lottery, possibly they spotted me Battle-scarred shogun, explosion when my pen hits tremendous, ultra-violet shine blind forensics

-Wu-Tang Clan, “Triumph,” Wu-Tang Forever
