Hanyang Animation Production Reflection

I created animated and gamified lessons while teaching at Hanyang University Elementary School. The projects had to work in a live classroom, support young English learners, and give students an immediate reason to participate. That shaped both the tools I chose and the way I built each project.

Choosing the tools

PowerPoint was the best authoring tool for the classroom because it was already installed, worked reliably with the classroom projector, and let me revise a lesson quickly. I used animation timing, motion paths, triggers, hyperlinks, layered images, audio, and video to control what students saw and when they saw it. Camtasia was better when I needed a recorded performance, tighter audio control, compositing, or a finished video that could play without live input. Flash gave me another way to build timeline-based animation and combine visual and audio assets.

Three production examples

  • Pokémon Training: I built a continuing classroom game with maps, routes, experience points, badges, choices, dice, encounters, and progress tracking. The presentation uses hundreds of timed animation actions along with click-based navigation and embedded media.
  • DDT sequence: I used custom motion paths to show how DDT moves through a food chain. Movement made the relationship between the organisms easier to follow than a static diagram.
  • The Three Little Pigs: I adapted written and illustrated lesson material into a timed audiovisual sequence. I combined presenter footage, changing lyrics, story images, audio editing, and final video exports.

Requirements, challenges, and revision

Classroom pacing was the main design constraint. Students needed enough time to understand the English, make a choice, and respond, while the activity still had to move quickly enough to hold their attention. I separated information into short reveals, used repeated visual rules, and made progress visible through maps, levels, badges, and rewards. I also tested the projects during instruction and revised confusing sequences, slow transitions, and navigation problems as they appeared.

Production process

My process started with the learning goal and the classroom activity. I wrote the story or interaction sequence, gathered or created the visual and audio assets, assembled the slides or timeline, animated the sequence, tested the controls and pacing, revised the project after classroom use, and prepared the final classroom or video version. The surviving source files, organized asset folders, revised lesson versions, Camtasia project, and exported videos document those stages.

Authorship note: I created the presentations, animation, interaction design, timing, game rules, sequencing, editing, and classroom implementation. Some recognizable character art and the Three Little Pigs illustrations come from the media properties or curriculum materials used in the lessons.
